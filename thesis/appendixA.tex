\section{Cross-Species Generalization and Proteomic Complexity}

The empirical results in Table~\ref{tab:species_stratification} reveal consistent performance across diverse evolutionary taxa, supporting several key findings:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item \textbf{Microbial and Fungal Proteomes:} Unicellular organisms (\emph{S. cerevisiae} and \emph{B. subtilis}) exhibit the highest sequence reconstruction fidelity, reaching 30.52\% and 26.46\% strict exact match (72.44\% and 65.78\% under isobaric I/L match) and over 83\% residue F1 score. This reflects well-characterized tryptic cleavage profiles and lower frequencies of non-canonical post-translational modifications.
\item \textbf{Plant and Extremophile Proteomes:} Accuracy remains robust across plant species (\emph{S. lycopersicum} at 67.14\% I/L match, 80.49\% residue F1; \emph{V. mungo} at 61.44\% I/L match, 81.64\% residue F1) as well as the archaeal methanogen \emph{M. mazei} (66.76\% I/L match) and anaerobic bacterium \emph{C. endoloripes} (64.62\% I/L match, 83.24\% residue F1). This confirms that DFlowNovo learns invariant physical fragmentation pathways rather than memorizing species-specific peptide distributions.
\item \textbf{Mammalian Proteomic Dynamics and Isobaric Divergence:} On mammalian proteomes (\emph{M. musculus} and \emph{H. sapiens}), strict exact match is lower (19.94\% and 14.12\%), but expands dramatically to 54.46\% and 62.92\% under isobaric I/L equivalence (a +48.80\% jump on human peptides). This verifies that mammalian sequencing errors are predominantly driven by the high prevalence of Leucine and Isoleucine residues ($113.084\,\text{Da}$), which cannot be resolved by standard collision-induced dissociation.
\end{enumerate}
