\chapter{Empirical Benchmark Evaluations and Quantitative Comparisons}

To rigorously assess the performance of DFlowNovo against state-of-the-art \emph{de novo} peptide sequencing architectures, we conducted extensive evaluations on two massive, standardized proteomics benchmarks totaling over 369,000 test spectra. This chapter presents quantitative accuracy comparisons, in-depth descriptions of the recorded evaluation metrics, precision-coverage dynamics, latency scaling measurements, and ablation experiments.

\section{Benchmark Datasets and Standardized Evaluation Protocol}

The framework was evaluated across two gold-standard mass spectrometry benchmarks totaling 369,532 test spectra, acquired directly from the official public Hugging Face repositories published by InstaDeep:
\begin{enumerate}[(1)]
\item \textbf{Nine-Species Biological Benchmark (\texttt{InstaDeepAI/ms\_ninespecies\_benchmark}):} Curated originally by \citet{Tran2017} and packaged for machine learning ingestion by InstaDeep on the Hugging Face Hub (\url{https://huggingface.co/datasets/InstaDeepAI/ms_ninespecies_benchmark}), this benchmark comprises $N = 104,163$ high-resolution test spectra spanning nine evolutionary divergent taxa across the tree of life: \emph{Bacillus subtilis}, \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} (Yeast), \emph{Vigna mungo} (Black gram), \emph{Methanosarcina mazei} (Archaea), \emph{Candidatus Endoloripes}, \emph{Apis mellifera} (Honeybee), \emph{Solanum lycopersicum} (Tomato), \emph{Mus musculus} (Mouse), and \emph{Homo sapiens} (Human). While the underlying raw experimental acquisitions originate from the UCSD MassIVE repository (Accession: \texttt{MSV000081382}) and ProteomeXchange (Accession: \texttt{PXD004948}), our training and evaluation pipelines loaded the standardized partitions directly from InstaDeep's Hugging Face repository. Evaluating across multiple phylogenetically distant organisms rigorously assesses generalizability to unseen biological proteomes without genomic memorization.
\item \textbf{ProteomeTools HC-PT Synthetic Benchmark (\texttt{InstaDeepAI/ms\_proteometools}):} Generated by \citet{Zolg2017} and released as a standardized machine learning dataset by InstaDeep on the Hugging Face Hub (\url{https://huggingface.co/datasets/InstaDeepAI/ms_proteometools}), this benchmark comprises $N = 265,369$ high-confidence test spectra acquired on synthetic human peptides using Orbitrap mass spectrometers \citep{Makarov2000, Zubarev2007} under standardized Higher-Energy Collisional Dissociation (HCD) \citep{Olsen2007} across stepped normalized collision energies. Although the raw physical acquisitions reside in the EMBL-EBI PRIDE repository (Accession: \texttt{PXD004732}) and UCSD MassIVE (Accessions: \texttt{MSV000080502} and \texttt{MSV000084000}), our models were trained and benchmarked directly on the pre-processed Hugging Face dataset published by InstaDeep.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Evaluation Metrics and Biophysical Importance}

Following established conventions in computational proteomics \citep{Tran2017, Yilmaz2022, Eloff2025}, predicted peptide sequences $\hat{Y} = (\hat{y}_1, \dots, \hat{y}_{\hat{L}})$ are evaluated against ground-truth sequences $Y = (y_1, \dots, y_L)$ using seven quantitative metrics. Each metric captures a distinct operational or biophysical dimension of the sequencing task:

\subsection{Residue-Level Precision, Recall, and F1 Score}

A predicted residue $\hat{y}_j$ is defined as a true positive if its cumulative prefix mass $\sum_{i=1}^j m(\hat{y}_i)$ matches a ground-truth prefix mass $\sum_{k=1}^l m(y_k)$ within a residue mass tolerance of $\tau_{\text{aa}} = 0.1\,\text{Da}$ and a cumulative tolerance of $\tau_{\text{cum}} = 0.5\,\text{Da}$ \citep{Tran2017}:
\begin{align}
\text{Residue Precision} &= \frac{\text{Total True Positive Residues}}{\text{Total Predicted Residues Across Dataset}} \label{eq:aa_precision} \\
\text{Residue Recall} &= \frac{\text{Total True Positive Residues}}{\text{Total Ground Truth Residues Across Dataset}} \label{eq:aa_recall} \\
\text{Residue F1} &= \frac{2 \times \text{Residue Precision} \times \text{Residue Recall}}{\text{Residue Precision} + \text{Residue Recall}} \label{eq:aa_f1}
\end{align}
\textbf{Importance:} In mass spectrometry, fragmentation spectra frequently contain missing internal cleavage peaks. A model might fail to predict the complete sequence while successfully recovering long contiguous sub-peptides (e.g., matching 12 out of 14 residues). Residue-level metrics quantify this partial ladder reconstruction fidelity. Residue precision measures the reliability of predicted amino acids, residue recall measures coverage across the true proteome, and the F1 score prevents models from artificially inflating precision by outputting truncated sequences.

\subsection{Strict Exact Match ($\mathbb{I}(\hat{Y} = Y)$)}

The strict exact match requires exact character-by-character string equality over the entire sequence:
\begin{equation}
\text{Strict Match} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^N \mathbb{I}(\hat{Y}^{(n)} = Y^{(n)})
\label{eq:strict_match}
\end{equation}
\textbf{Importance:} Strict exact match is the primary metric for applications in synthetic biology, recombinant antibody validation, and clinical oncology (neoantigen identification). In these domains, a single mutated or substituted amino acid can alter the tertiary folding of a protein, ablate enzymatic catalytic activity, or destroy MHC class I/II binding affinity.

\subsection{Isobaric I/L Match ($\mathbb{I}(\hat{Y}_{\text{I}\to\text{L}} = Y_{\text{I}\to\text{L}})$)}

The isobaric match treats constitutional isomers Leucine (L) and Isoleucine (I) as equivalent:
\begin{equation}
\text{I/L Match} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^N \mathbb{I}(\hat{Y}_{\text{I}\to\text{L}}^{(n)} = Y_{\text{I}\to\text{L}}^{(n)})
\label{eq:il_match}
\end{equation}
\textbf{Importance:} As analyzed in Chapter 5, Leucine and Isoleucine share the identical molecular formula ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{13}\text{NO}_2$) and monoisotopic residue mass ($113.084064\,\text{Da}$). Under standard collision-induced dissociation (HCD), they produce identical $b$- and $y$-ion series. Discrepancies between L and I reflect an insurmountable physical boundary of low-energy MS/MS rather than algorithmic failure. Tracking I/L match isolates true algorithmic sequencing ability from instrument-imposed physical ambiguity.

\subsection{Accepted Coverage at Fixed Precision Thresholds}

In high-throughput proteomics workflows, spectra are filtered by model confidence to satisfy a specified False Discovery Rate (FDR) \citep{Elias2007}, typically corresponding to $\ge 80\%$, $90\%$, or $95\%$ residue precision:
\begin{equation}
\text{Coverage@}P^* = \frac{|\{n \in \{1, \dots, N\} \mid \text{Score}(\hat{Y}^{(n)}) \ge \gamma(P^*)\}|}{N}
\label{eq:coverage_threshold}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma(P^*)$ is the score cutoff threshold at which cumulative residue precision across accepted spectra equals $P^*$.
\textbf{Importance:} In biological research, total identification yield at high confidence is the defining practical metric. A sequencing model that achieves high accuracy on only the top 10\% easiest spectra is of limited clinical utility. Measuring coverage at 80\% precision directly quantifies how many biological peptides an analytical laboratory can reliably report from an experimental run.

\subsection{Average Precision (Area Under the Precision-Coverage Curve)}

Average Precision (AP) computes the numerical integral of the precision-coverage curve:
\begin{equation}
\text{AP} = \int_0^1 \text{Precision}(c) \, \mathrm{d}c
\label{eq:average_precision}
\end{equation}
\textbf{Importance:} AP evaluates global confidence calibration and ranking quality across all possible operating thresholds without relying on an arbitrary single cutoff.

\subsection{Precursor Mass Error (PPM)}

The precursor mass error measures the normalized deviation of the predicted peptide mass:
\begin{equation}
\Delta m_{\text{ppm}} = \frac{|\sum_{i=1}^{\hat{L}} m(\hat{y}_i) - (M_{\text{prec}} - M_{\text{H}_2\text{O}})|}{M_{\text{prec}}} \times 10^6
\label{eq:ppm_error}
\end{equation}
\textbf{Importance:} Physical mass conservation dictates that the sum of constituent residue masses must match the parent intact ion mass. High-resolution Orbitrap mass spectrometers operate at $< 10\text{--}20\,\text{ppm}$ accuracy \citep{Makarov2000, Zubarev2007}. Evaluating the standard deviation $\sigma(\Delta m_{\text{ppm}})$ demonstrates whether the generative model obeys physical mass conservation or hallucinates chemically impossible sequence candidates.

\subsection{Inference Throughput (Spectra/s) and Wall-Clock Latency}

Inference throughput measures the number of complete tandem spectra sequenced per second on a single GPU.
\textbf{Importance:} Modern liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) runs generate $60,000$ to $120,000$ MS2 spectra per hour, and clinical patient cohorts produce tens of millions of spectra. Autoregressive models processing 28--50 spectra per second require days to weeks of GPU compute, creating severe processing bottlenecks. High throughput enables real-time spectral processing aligned with instrument acquisition rates.

\section{Competitive Baseline Models}

We benchmark DFlowNovo against five leading computational sequencing paradigms:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item \textbf{InstaNovo v1.2.0} (94.8M parameters) \citep{Eloff2025}: Autoregressive Transformer trained on MassIVE-KB with mass-guided knapsack beam search.
\item \textbf{InstaNovo v1.0.0} (94.8M parameters) \citep{Eloff2025}: The initial release trained on ACPT synthetic data.
\item \textbf{Casanovo v5.2.1} (47.0M parameters) \citep{Yilmaz2022}: Deep autoregressive Transformer framing sequencing as machine translation with beam decoding.
\item \textbf{PowerNovo2} (63.2M parameters) \citep{Petrovskiy2026}: Continuous normalizing flow combining GLOW and ALPS continuous latent density estimators.
\item \textbf{PointNovo} (32.1M parameters) \citep{Qiao2021}: Order-invariant continuous spectrum encoder coupled with autoregressive decoding.
\item \textbf{DeepNovo} (28.4M parameters) \citep{Tran2017}: Convolutional spectrum encoder combined with bidirectional LSTM beam search.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Precision-Coverage Dynamics and Statistical Significance}

\begin{figure}[!ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{images/precision_coverage_benchmark.png}
\caption{Precision-coverage curves and accepted PSM analysis on the 104k Nine-Species benchmark. DFlowNovo accepts 86,412 peptide-spectrum matches at 80\% precision (+31,412 spectra compared to InstaNovo v1.0), maintaining superior precision at high coverage.}
\label{fig:precision_coverage_benchmark}
\end{figure}

As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:precision_coverage_benchmark}, DFlowNovo identifies \textbf{86,412 accepted spectra at 80\% precision} on Nine-Species, compared to 55,000 for InstaNovo v1.0 (+31,412 accepted PSMs, a 57.1\% increase in high-confidence biological identifications).

To verify that these accuracy improvements are statistically significant and not artifacts of stochastic sampling, we conducted McNemar's paired test with Edwards continuity correction \citep{Giguere2015} between DFlowNovo and InstaNovo on the full test sets:
\begin{equation}
\chi^2 = \frac{(|b - c| - 1)^2}{b + c}
\label{eq:mcnemar_test}
\end{equation}
where $b$ denotes cases correctly predicted by DFlowNovo but missed by InstaNovo, and $c$ denotes the converse. For Nine-Species ($b = 21,430$, $c = 6,854$), the test yields $\chi^2 = 7,542.8$ ($p < 10^{-15}$), confirming significant superiority at the 99.9\% confidence level.

\section{Inference Throughput and Latency Scaling}

A central advantage of non-autoregressive discrete flow matching is constant $\mathcal{O}(K)$ inference latency with respect to peptide length $L$. Figure~\ref{fig:sampling_dynamics_and_latency} presents the wall-clock latency analysis benchmarked across peptide lengths $L \in [7, 30]$ on an NVIDIA H100 SXM5 GPU.

\begin{figure}[!ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.88\textwidth]{images/sampling_dynamics_and_latency.png}
\caption{Inference latency and throughput scaling. (a) Per-spectrum decoding latency as a function of peptide length $L \in [7, 30]$. DFlowNovo maintains flat $\mathcal{O}(1)$ latency ($\approx 5.8\,\text{ms}$), whereas autoregressive models exhibit steep $\mathcal{O}(L)$ linear scaling. (b) Batched GPU throughput (spectra/second) under batch sizes 16 to 256. (c) Trade-off between flow integration steps $K$ and sequence accuracy. (d) GPU memory consumption during batched decoding.}
\label{fig:sampling_dynamics_and_latency}
\end{figure}

While autoregressive models (Casanovo, InstaNovo) scale linearly with length $\mathcal{O}(L)$---requiring up to $42.5\,\text{ms}$ per spectrum for long peptides ($L \ge 25$)---DFlowNovo generates all positions simultaneously, maintaining a constant latency of approximately $5.8\,\text{ms}$ across all lengths. Overall, DFlowNovo achieves \textbf{227.1 to 255.8 spectra/second}, representing a \textbf{$4.33\times$ to $4.94\times$ speedup} over InstaNovo ($51.9\,\text{spec/s}$) and an \textbf{$8.0\times$ to $9.0\times$ speedup} over Casanovo ($28.5\,\text{spec/s}$).
